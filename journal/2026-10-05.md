# 2026-10-05

## Discussed / Decided
- We switched the electricity data from energy-charts to SMARD (Bundesnetzagentur).
- Our data now starts on 20 Jan 2024 instead of 1 Jan 2024.
- We added a seasonal check. As well as comparing each fortnight's SHAP ranking with Oct 2025, we also compare it with the same fortnight in 2025 (tau_seasonal).
- The single window 2 SD result stays as our main result, but we also report a "two windows in a row" version as a robustness check.
- Both models trained fine. MAE on Oct 2025 was 14.5 €/MWh for the blind model and 14.9 for the oil model, against 43.5 for a naive "same as yesterday" forecast. Training MAE is about 11, so the gap is about 3.5, and early stopping kicked in at about 80 trees, so the models aren't overfitting.
- Blind model: tau never crossed its threshold. MAE crossed on 20 Jun (4 Jul with two in a row). So the explanations gave no early warning, the error just went up.
- Oil model: tau crossed once, on 25 Apr, 56 days before MAE. But it fails the two in a row check, and in that same window tau_seasonal went the other way. The top feature in that window was solar, where in October it was wind. So it was spring solar, not the oil shock, and it's a false alarm.
- PSI was over 0.25 for at least one feature in every window, even before the shock, so as we've built it PSI doesn't tell us anything.
- By September yesterday's price had overtaken wind as the top feature in both models, so the explanations did change eventually, just slowly and not before the error went up.
- Plots are in figures/ (drift_blind.png, drift_oil.png, drift_*_seasonal.png) and numbers are in results/summary.md and results/seasonal_check.md.

## Why
- The energy-charts API gave 503 errors for over 24 hours and the slides are due Friday, so we couldn't wait. SMARD has the same DE-LU series under the same licence (CC BY 4.0), so nothing about the method had to change. The other option was to show the method on made up data only.
- The temperature archive only starts on 20 Jan 2024. Losing 3 weeks out of 21 months was better than dropping temperature as a feature, since it affects demand.
- After the shock, tau actually went down in March and April, meaning the explanations looked more normal, not less. That made us think we were seeing the change of season rather than the shock. Comparing with October can't tell those apart, but comparing with the same fortnight a year earlier mostly takes the season out.
- One fortnight over the line could just be noise, especially with only 8 baseline windows, so a warning that only lasts one window shouldn't count as an early warning.
- The 56 day lead looked like our headline result at first, but after the two in a row and seasonal checks we don't believe it anymore. Better to catch that now than in the Q&A.
- PSI compares one fortnight against 21 months of mixed seasons, so it will always look different.
- Things we caught:
  - SMARD's docs list the wrong ID for solar. 126 is actually wind (negated) plus solar, and 125 is solar.
  - SMARD's 15 minute values are MWh, not MW, so averaging them to hourly would have been wrong. We download hourly data instead.
  - The temperature feature is already a 2 day ahead forecast, so lagging it again would have been wrong.
  - The first leakage test flagged yesterday's price, but that's fine, D-1 prices are set on D-2 so we'd know them in time.
  - The old README said SHAP is compared to the baseline. It's actually compared to Oct 2025, the baseline only sets the 2 SD thresholds.
