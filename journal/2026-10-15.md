# 2026-10-15

## Discussed / Decided
- We did our presentation on Monday. Since then we've done the two code fixes from the next steps in our last entry and reran everything.
- Early stopping now has its own month. Training runs from Jan 2024 to Aug 2025, Sep 2025 decides when to stop adding trees, and Oct 2025 is only used to check accuracy.
- PSI now compares each fortnight with the same fortnight a year earlier, instead of with all of training.
- We downloaded fresh data, so we now have 16 fortnights after the shock instead of 15. Before changing the code we checked the old version still gave the same results on the new data, so the changes below come from the two fixes.
- The models now stop at 377 trees (blind) and 520 (oil) instead of about 80. MAE on Oct 2025 is 15.4 €/MWh for the blind model and 16.3 for the oil model, against 43.5 for the naive forecast, so still about two-thirds better.
- Blind model: tau crossed once, on 25 Apr, and was back under the next fortnight. MAE crossed on 20 Jun (4 Jul with two in a row), and by the newest fortnight the error was over three times its normal level.
- Oil model: tau crossed on 25 Apr and stayed over on 9 May, so it now passes the two in a row check, 56 days before MAE.
- tau_seasonal never crossed in either model, and the top feature in both 25 Apr windows was solar. Solar was top in almost every fortnight from late March to mid August.
- PSI now only crosses in mid August (blind) and mid September (oil), well after the error went up.
- Yesterday's price was in the top 3 in all 16 fortnights after the shock in both models. Before the shock it was only in the top 3 once out of 8 (blind) and never (oil). It's mostly 2nd or 3rd though, behind solar or wind.
- Our answer stays the same: once the season is taken out, the explanations don't drift before the error does.

## Why
- Before, October was used both to decide when to stop and to say how accurate the models were, so the accuracy was a bit flattering. Now October is a fair test, so a slightly worse MAE is expected.
- September keeps improving with more trees for longer than October did, probably because it comes straight after the training data and looks a lot like it. That's why the tree count jumped, and why the models now fit the training data more closely.
- PSI flagged everything before because it compared one fortnight with 21 months of every season mixed together. Comparing with the same time last year takes the season out.
- Last time we ruled out the oil model's warning because it only lasted one fortnight and because it disappeared against the same time last year. Only the second reason still holds now, but with solar on top it still looks like spring, not the shock. So the seasonal check is now the main thing stopping us from reading it as an early warning.
- PSI works now, but it still warned after the error, so watching the inputs didn't give an early warning either.
- We think the model leans on yesterday's price more after the shock because when prices are high and jumpy, "tomorrow will look like today" is its best guess, and the blind model has no other way to see fuel costs. Tau misses it because it stays behind solar and wind.
