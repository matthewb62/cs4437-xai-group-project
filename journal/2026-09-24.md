# 2026-09-24

## Discussed / Decided
- We researched data sources for the model.
- Initially checked Ireland, which has relatively weak data, then looked for stronger sources, checking different countries in Europe and settling on Germany, for the moment anyways!
- Germany has hourly data for both electricity prices and traffic, up to 4 months after the incident, making it a stronger choice than other country's data. Germany also has clearer licensing for data usage!

## Why
- We need to gather regular data from January 2024 to January 2026. And then we need to gather data from the incident (Febuary 2026), to as recently as possible. Electricity data is perfectly fine for this, as it is published daily(!). The traffic domain is trickier, as it is published later. For post-incidenct traffic data, we only have from Febuary to June, not up until today.
- The data also needs to be hourly or daily. We're planning to check the models reasoning every fortnight or month, so we need enough data points in each of those windows. Germany's is hourly for both, which is plenty.
- All the German data can be downloaded directly. The electricity prices come from a free online service, and the traffic data comes as monthly zip files, which might be awkward compared to other countries (still open to change!) but fine.
- Both German datasets say they're free to reuse as long as we credit them (a licence called CC BY 4.0).
- The UK was a close second. Its data covers the same dates as Germany's and is probably a bit easier to work with, but we couldn't find anything saying we're allowed to use the UK traffic data.
- Why not Ireland:
  - The Irish traffic data only goes up to the end of March 2026, so just one month after the incident.
  - Dublin City Council's traffic data doesn't have anything from 2026 yet.
  - The Irish traffic website probably has more recent numbers, but it blocks automatic downloads.
  - The Irish electricity price website only keeps the last year or so, so there's nothing from 2024.
- We also found a daily oil price (Brent crude) to feed into the model, so it "knows" about the incident. You can see the jump clearly: about $71 a barrel at the end of February, then $121 by the end of March.

## Still Open
- We can 100% change from Germany. If we'd rather stick with Ireland, we'd only have one month of traffic data after the incident.
- Third domain: flights could work. Eurocontrol has daily flight numbers for every European airport (Dublin included!) from 2019 up to the end of August 2026. This ties in with the cancelled flights idea from last entry. We still need to check whether we're allowed to use it.
- We only have about 4 months of traffic data after the incident. If we check the model every fortnight, that's about 8 checks, but only 4 if we do it monthly. So fortnightly is probably better. More German traffic data might come out before the deadline, but we can't count on it.
- Forecast horizon is still undecided. Day-ahead makes sense for electricity. For traffic, maybe week-ahead?
- We could only find a monthly gas price, which isn't detailed enough to use. We might just use the oil price on its own.
- The data will need some cleaning:
  - The German electricity prices changed from hourly to every 15 minutes at some point, so we'll have to convert them back to hourly.
  - The German traffic files are in their own format, not a normal spreadsheet, so we'll need to work out how to read them.
