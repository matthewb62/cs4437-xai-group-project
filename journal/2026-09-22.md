# 2026-09-22

## Discussed / Decided
- Landed on a topic: "whether a model's explanations shift before its accuracy drops when the world changes underneath it". We plan to test this on the oil crisis / shock which happened this year due to the Strait of Hormuz closure. We plan to look at various different domains such as electricity prices, traffic and maybe a third one we are yet to decide on. 

-The rough plan is train a model on pre-shock data, run SHAP monthly / fortnightly through 2026 and measure how far the feature rankings have moved from baseline, then plot that against error rate. The question is which line moves first.

## Why
- We went through a load of options at first. Decided against a pure critique paper (chain of thought faithfulness) because we figured we would learn more building something. Decided againt SHAP vs LIME on a specific dataset as it was an obvious pick. Decided against a planted bias test for SHAP vs LIME too as we thought of this idea. 

- We settled on the oil shock for a few reasons: its recent, its well documented, and analysts genuinely got it wrong. The predictions were exponentially increasing gas prices and cancelled flights, but this wasnt the case. If experts misread it, maybe there were clues / hints in the explanations of the models? Thats what we set to find out.
