## Discussed / Decided
- We decided to make Electricity our sole domain and to drop traffic and flights. 
- We settled on training the model from Jan 2024 - Sep 2025, we're going to validate it against Oct 2025, and then emit Nov 2025 - mid Feb 2026 as a pre crisis baseline. Will test from late Feb 2026 onward.
- While we are only going to have one domain we are going to have 2 model versions, a main "blind" model with no oil price, and a comparison model that's the same but also gets the oil price. We originally planned to have oil in the main model, but switched after realising a model that can't see the crisis fits our question better.
- We're going to compare SHAP against Population Stability Index (PSI).
- We're going to use LightGBM as our model and predict 'tomorrow's' 24 hourly prices (a day-ahead forecast), and run SHAP on it every fortnight.
- To measure how much the explanations have drifted we'll use Kendall's tau, which compares each fortnight's SHAP feature ranking to the baseline ranking. If it drops further than normal (more than 2 standard deviations from the pre crisis window) we count it as a warning. We'll use the same 2 standard deviation rule to decide when the model's error has risen enough to count as an accuracy drop.
- In the comparison model, we're going to feed oil in as a 30 day % change instead of the raw price.
- Features in the main model: past prices (1, 2 and 7 days ago), electricity demand, wind and solar forecasts, gas-fired generation, temperature, and time features (hour, weekday, month, holidays). The comparison model adds the oil price.
- We dropped the gas price as a feature
- We checked the energy-charts day-ahead forecasts for demand, solar, wind onshore and wind offshore, and they all go back to Jan 2024, so we can use forecasts instead of actual amounts. They're in 15 minute steps, so we'll convert them to hourly like the prices.

## Why
- The project is quite short and trying to achieve too much could easily backfire, focusing on just electricity is the safer bet. Also, traffic data stops in June, giving us only about 8 checks, not as much as we would like. 
- The data we leave out from Nov to mid Feb we can use to measure normal SHAP wobble, so we can distinguish between real drift and just noise.
- The main model doesn't know about the crisis at all, which fits our question better, we want to see if its explanations shift just from the electricity data changing. The comparison model with oil tells us whether giving it the crisis directly changes the result.
- A PSI comparison will show whether SHAP is adding anything we couldnt tell from just watching the inputs change.
- LightGBM is one of the best models for table-style data, and SHAP works really well with it to get exact values, and fast.
- Day-ahead matches how the German electricity market works, tomorrow's prices are set in an auction today.
- 2 standard deviations is a common, fairly strict threshold, so normal wobble shouldn't set off false warnings.
- Models like LightGBM can't handle values bigger than anything they saw in training, so $120 oil would just be treated like the highest price it's seen. A % change helps, but the ~70% jump in March is still bigger than anything in training. We're accepting this as a limitation, it means the comparison model under-reacts to oil, so any drift it shows is probably an underestimate.
- We use wind and solar forecasts rather than the actual amounts because the forecasts are what was known when prices were set.
- The only daily gas price we found (Yahoo Finance) isn't openly licensed, and oil already covers the crisis in the comparison model, so we dropped it.
