We asked whether a model's explanations drift before its accuracy does.
Under our single-window rule they did, by up to 56 days. The signal
is fragile, however. When two windows in a row are required, the blind
model's $\tau_w$ never warns while its error does, and $\tau_w$ measured
against the same fortnight a year earlier never crosses for either model,
suggesting much of the early movement was seasonal. Rank-based explanation
drift is therefore not yet a reliable early warning here. The explanations
changed more in scale than in order. Attribution to yesterday's price
tripled by September, while the
same four features stayed on top, growth that a rank-based measure cannot
register.

Our study has clear limits. It covers one event, one market and one model type (gradient-boosted
trees), so the results may not generalise. The models are frozen, so we
cannot say how monitoring interacts with retraining. Links between drift
and world events are plausible but untested. The thresholds rest on only
eight baseline windows, a small sample for a standard deviation. We used
only TreeSHAP with a mean-$|$SHAP$|$ ranking. Other explainers may behave
differently.

Future work should test drift measures that track magnitude as well as
rank, use seasonally matched reference rankings by default, and study
monitoring alongside scheduled retraining.
