We asked whether a model's explanations drift before its accuracy does.
Under our single-window rule they did: for both models, weighted Kendall's
$\tau$ first crossed its warning threshold on 25 April 2026, 56 days before
MAE for the blind model and 28 days before it for the oil model. The signal
is fragile, however. When two windows in a row are required, the blind
model's $\tau$ never warns while its error does, and $\tau$ measured against
the same fortnight a year earlier never crosses for either model, suggesting
much of the early movement was seasonal. Rank-based explanation drift is
therefore not yet a reliable early warning here. The explanations changed
more in scale than in order: the mean absolute SHAP value of yesterday's
price rose from 11.3 to 35.3~EUR/MWh by September, while the same four
features stayed on top, growth that a rank-based measure cannot register.

Our study has clear limits. It covers one event, one market and one model type (gradient-boosted
trees), so the results may not generalise. The models are frozen, so we
cannot say how monitoring interacts with retraining. Links between drift
and world events are plausible but untested. The thresholds rest on only
eight baseline windows, a small sample for a standard deviation. The oil
model met 30-day Brent moves of up to 77.6\%, far beyond the 21.4\% seen in
training, and tree models cannot extrapolate, so its response is likely
understated. We used only TreeSHAP with a mean-$|$SHAP$|$ ranking; other
explainers may behave differently. Finally, SHAP explains the model, not
the market: it shows which inputs drove a prediction, not why prices moved.

Future work should test drift measures that track magnitude as well as
rank, use seasonally matched reference rankings by default, and study
monitoring alongside scheduled retraining.
