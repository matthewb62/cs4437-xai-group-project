Forecasting models are usually monitored through their error, known only
once the true outcome arrives. SHAP explanations exist as soon as a
prediction is made, so changes in them could warn earlier. We test this
through a real shock with a known date, the closure of the Strait of Hormuz
on 28 February 2026. Two frozen LightGBM models forecast German day-ahead
electricity prices, one without fuel prices and one given the 30-day change
in Brent. Each fortnight we rank features by mean absolute TreeSHAP value,
compare the ranking with a pre-shock reference using weighted Kendall's tau,
and apply a two-standard-deviation warning rule to tau, error and the
Population Stability Index. Tau warned up to 56 days before error, but a
seasonally matched comparison traces this to the change of season rather
than the shock. The explanations changed more in size than in order, as
attribution to yesterday's price tripled, which a rank measure barely
registers. In this single event, explanations are better monitored
alongside error than in place of it.
