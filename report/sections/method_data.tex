We use hourly day-ahead prices for the German--Luxembourg (DE-LU) bidding
zone from SMARD\footnote{\url{https://www.smard.de}}, with its day-ahead
load, wind and solar forecasts \cite{trebbien2023} and actual gas-fired
generation, plus Open-Meteo temperature forecasts and daily Brent prices
from FRED. All series use Berlin time. Gaps of up to three hours in the
hourly inputs are interpolated; the price is never filled.

The data are split by date, so the model is never evaluated on hours it
has seen. Both models are trained on 20 January 2024 to 31 August 2025
(14,140 hours), early-stopped on September 2025 and validated on October
2025.

Every feature for hour $h$ on day $D$ must have been known before the
auction closed at 12:00 on $D-1$. Prices are lagged to the same hour on
$D-1$, $D-2$ and $D-7$. Gas generation is taken from $D-1$ for hours
before noon and from $D-2$ after. Temperature is a two-day-ahead forecast
and Brent the last close on or before $D-2$. The pipeline checks every
lagged timestamp against this cut-off. Hour, weekday, month and a
public-holiday flag complete the inputs.

Two models differ in one column: the \emph{blind} model never sees a fuel
price, while the \emph{oil} model adds the 30-day change in Brent. Both
share rows, hyperparameters and seed. Each is a single LightGBM regressor
\cite{ke2017} covering all 24 hours, with learning rate 0.05 and 31 leaves.

On October 2025, the blind and oil models reach an MAE of 15.4 and
16.3~EUR/MWh, against 43.5 for a seasonal naive forecast using the price
one week earlier \cite{lago2021}.
